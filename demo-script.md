# Demo for the pitch (about 3 minutes) — rewritten 30 Oct

Pages now: Watching · My shelf · Changes · Books & videos · About, plus the Account menu (Your account, Your vault,
Feedback inbox). Stats is linked at the bottom of every page. The summary + email settings are at the top of Changes.

## Before the pitch (do these in order, today)

1. **A clean pitch account.** Make a new account just for the demo (so your real one stays private). On Watching,
   press "Try it with real examples". It adds two Wikipedia articles per theme (with 90 days of real edits), a video,
   and the book comparisons (Frankenstein 1818 vs 1831, Darwin 1859 vs 1872). Give the books a few minutes to finish.
2. **Pick your star change.** Open Changes and find one real change about an Indigenous community article that's easy to
   understand at a glance (ideally a name spelled differently, or a removed passage). Note which one.
3. **Pre-run one "Test against Kiwix".** On a Wikipedia example in Watching, press Test against Kiwix and check the result
   makes sense (the Basotho one went wrong before; delete any bad result with the button at its bottom).
4. **My shelf:** keep a copy of one link (it gets a stamp of originality), and lock ONE harmless item in your vault
   (e.g. a note saying "Gazette notice 12, kept privately"). Never use anything real or sensitive on screen.
5. **Feedback:** lock the feedback box (Account → Feedback inbox). Ask your friend to try Tripwire and send feedback
   (message below). Pick one quote, and if you change something because of it, say so in the pitch: that's the strongest
   "iteration" evidence there is.
6. **Email:** press "Email it to me now" (top of Changes) so a summary is sitting in your inbox to show.
7. **Two windows, ready to go:**
   - A private/incognito window with the site's address typed in, NOT yet loaded: the opening plays on a fresh visit.
   - Your pitch account, with tabs already open: the star change, the Kiwix result, the Frankenstein result, the shelf
     item with its stamp, the locked vault item, and your email inbox.
8. **A backup video (2-3 minutes)** of the demo, in case the Wi-Fi fails. Mac: Cmd+Shift+5. Windows: Windows+Alt+R.
   Phone: the screen recorder in the quick settings.
9. **Just before:** Do Not Disturb on, other tabs closed, browser zoomed to 125% so the back row can read it, charger in.

## How it maps to the judging
- **Value (30%):** name the people; show a real change on a real Indigenous-community article; your tester's quote.
- **Craft (25%):** the deliberate choices: no AI, ask twice before public copies, summaries not pings, name spellings,
  the vault, anonymous locked feedback, random usernames, honest numbers, slow on purpose.
- **Evidence (30%):** it's live; real changes caught; the real email; over 370 automatic tests on two kinds of database.
- **Iteration (15%):** About → How Tripwire changed, plus what changed because of your tester.

## If something breaks live
- Wi-Fi down: play the backup video, and keep talking over it.
- A page is slow: "Tripwire is slow on purpose", and switch to the next tab you already opened.
- The opening already played in that window: add `/?intro` to the address to replay it.

## Don't claim
- That Kiwix tests always work (they depend on Kiwix's and the Internet Archive's servers; you pre-checked one).
- That TikTok's "sensitive" labels can be seen (TikTok doesn't show them publicly).
- That the vault protects against everything: say "locked on your device; Tripwire can't read it", and that's exactly true.
