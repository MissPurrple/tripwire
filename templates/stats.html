Where these numbers come from, and what they miss

Nobody has a list of every book ever published. So this shows two different views:

  • Open Library (the Internet Archive's free catalogue): real, checkable counts of books by the year they were first published, and whether a scan or ebook exists (“digital copy”) or not (“print only”). It leans towards books held in Western libraries.
  • World estimate: in 2010 Google combined about a billion records from 150+ library catalogues, ISBN databases and booksellers, removed duplicates and non-books, and arrived at {{ '{:,}'.format(google_count) }} different books (each edition counted separately). From there, the estimate adds {{ '{:,}'.format(per_year_low) }} to {{ '{:,}'.format(per_year_high) }} new titles a year: the lower figure is UNESCO's estimate of recorded titles, the higher one includes self-published books with ISBNs. The middle line is the average of the two.
  • Books no one recorded. Many books never get an ISBN or a library record: printed locally and sold by hand, as often happens in Lesotho and many other places. They're in none of these numbers, so the real total is higher than every line here.
