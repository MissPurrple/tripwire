# Demo walkthrough for the pitch (about 3 minutes)

## Before the pitch (once)
1. In Vercel: set the email settings (README, "Weekly summary by email") and ADMIN_EMAILS = your account email. Redeploy.
2. On the live site, log in to your pitch account, open About, press "Add the examples to my account". Let the Frankenstein comparison finish.
3. Optional: in the Frankenstein results or a big Wikipedia change, keep ONE public copy (so the Archive tab has something). Remember: it's public and permanent.
4. Press "Email it to me now" on Weekly summary so the email is in your inbox to show.
5. Record a 2-3 minute screen video of the steps below, as a backup if Wi-Fi fails.
6. Open the Feedback inbox: pick 1-2 quotes from testers, and anything you changed because of them.

## The demo
1. HOME (15 s): the box computer boots up. "Tripwire watches the record of the world, and slows you down a little, on purpose."
2. WATCHING -> CHANGES (45 s): a real Wikipedia article about an Indigenous community, changed in the last 90 days. Open it: red/green before and after, who edited, Wikipedia's own permanent copies. Point out a name-spelling flag if there is one.
3. PUBLIC COPY (20 s): "Is this important to you?" -> the "public and permanent" screen. Explain why it asks twice (sacred/private knowledge). Don't press yes live unless you want to.
4. BOOKS (45 s): the "UNRESTRICTED KNOWLEDGE PRESERVATION" screen. Frankenstein 1818 vs 1831: the pie chart of change types, most interesting changes, word swaps, book facts. "Mary Shelley rewrote her own book; imagine what quiet rewrites happen without anyone noticing."
5. VIDEOS (15 s): a watched video; what Tripwire notices (removed, private, restricted, retitled).
6. STATS (20 s): humans vs bots right now; books published, Open Library vs the world estimate, and the honest note about books no one recorded (Lesotho).
7. WEEKLY EMAIL (10 s): show it in your inbox. "One calm email a week, not a ping for every change."
8. ABOUT (10 s): deliberate choices + how it changed + live totals + tester quotes.

## Lines for the rubric
- Value: who it's for (Indigenous communities and their documenters, independent journalists, storytellers, archivists).
- Craft: no AI on purpose; ask twice; weekly not pings; name spellings; 7-day deletion; own human check; honest numbers.
- Evidence: live link, live totals on About, 181 automatic tests on two kinds of database, the real email.
- Iteration: the timeline on About, plus what testers said.
- Be clear what's next (not built): browser button, live watching, Kiwix.
