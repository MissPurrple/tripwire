## 1. Original "ramble" for the buildathon profile (values)

I value cultural archiving and information preservation in the sense that I think it is important for us to ensure that the information that is with Indigenous communities is archived, preserved, and kept as valuable (because it is so, so important).

In the era we live in right now, AI is front and centre, and that means that digital information tends to be king. However, if you have people like me who try to veer towards being privacy-conscious, there are some people, or people of that ilk, who will very likely get into data poisoning, right? What does that mean for people? There are people who will, yes, get into data poisoning for their own personal privacy. There are people that I think, as humanity has proven that they are willing to do so, will likely get into data poisoning with the sole intention of perpetuating misinformation.

I would like to ensure that Indigenous knowledge does not just:
1. die with the people who have this knowledge if it is not documented
2. get preserved and archived in its most raw and most honest format, such that there is still a point of reference that cannot necessarily be prompt-injected with some poisoning, if you get what I mean.

## 2. From the community builders' dinner conversation (28 Oct 2026)

Given that spiel, I was having a conversation just a few hours ago at a community builders' dinner. We were talking about AI and how it feels like we're living in the beginning of the type of dystopian world that we were warned about in 1967, George Orwell, your Ready Player Ones, and your Matrix (those kinds of dystopian-type era movies and books).

It feels like that's what we're living in, especially if you're not a technical person. We're seeing so many threats, especially now in this month of October: security threats, etc. My biggest concern is the threat on information and, therefore, the threat on truth and what is perceived as the truth.

Somebody brought it up, I guess, because I was talking about this and about how, again, it is super ironic that I'm talking to you about it. I guess that's how this cookie is crumbling. We were talking about how a lot of the internet is now mostly made up of Asians and bots and AI in general as a whole, in all its different facets. You have a lot of old message boards and chat rooms that are no longer human-active but have now essentially been taken over by AI, agents, and bots.

Because of that, it feels like there is a part of human history that is being lost and is not being preserved. I am worried that not only is old information going to either die or be erased, but you can also have malicious people who go in and intentionally change this data or this information to say something else (so it can benefit them, which is what colonialism is literally all about).

I am also worried that there will not be enough information on Indigenous communities to the point where, once AI starts being given more power in terms of decisions that it can make, it will disregard Indigenous communities because it doesn't have enough information on them. Their customs, their traditions, their ideals, their belief systems, their philosophies, and their mindsets will not be taken into consideration as much as, say, the American mindset and systems would be (because they have a bunch of information about themselves online). Even though some of it is also a lie, it's there, versus what you have on more Indigenous and, I guess, obviously more African communities. That's what I worry about.

The whole chatbots taking over old chat rooms thing seems like something that could be fun to play around with, but I generally do not even know where I could even begin.
